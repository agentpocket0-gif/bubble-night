# Bubble Night UX Flow and Reference Review

## Research basis

This review uses publicly available Google Play listings and official support/community guidance checked on 4 November 2026. It records reusable interaction ideas, not a request to copy another game's artwork, branding, characters, sounds, or screen layouts.

Observed examples:

- **Block Blast!** is positioned around starting its core puzzle quickly and making each round easy to learn. Google Play listing: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.block.juggle
- **Bubble Witch 3 Saga** uses a clear bubble-shooter objective, an aiming guide, and introduces new mechanics progressively. Google Play listing: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.king.bubblewitch3
- **Toon Blast** keeps level goals and moves visible during play. Google Play listing: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.peakgames.toonblast
- **Bubble Shooter - Classic Pop** foregrounds drag-to-aim, match-colors, shoot, and offline play. Google Play listing: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=bubbleshooter.orig
- **Candy Crush Saga** currently opens directly to its Saga Map rather than inserting a separate main menu. Its help centre explains the Level Goals screen and optional pre-game boosters:
  - https://candycrush.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39931244875933-Where-can-I-find-my-settings-and-account-options
  - https://candycrush.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000754897-Which-game-modes-will-I-find
- King Community's Bubble Witch 3 beginner guide describes the active/next bubble selector, shot counter, and star-based level rewards: https://community.king.com/en/bubble-witch-saga/discussion/246620/bubble-witch-3-saga-beginners-guide

These observations inform navigation clarity, visible objectives, progressive teaching, and fewer unnecessary taps. They do not establish that every region or game version has identical screens.

## Bubble Night screen journey

1. **Android launch splash**  
   Android 12+ uses the OS splash screen with the app's adaptive icon and the dark night background. Older Android versions use the app's dark window background before Compose draws.

2. **Real loading state**  
   The app shows its branded loading card only while the local catalogue/profile is loading or another real operation is running. There is no artificial delay.

3. **Campaign Map first**  
   After initial loading, the player lands on the orbit map. The map highlights the next playable level, shows Easy / Medium / Hard, completion progress, earned stars, and locked nodes. New profiles default to Easy; existing saved difficulty choices are preserved.

4. **Resume or choose a level**  
   If a campaign session is saved, the map's main action resumes it. Otherwise the main action opens the current level. Players can also tap another unlocked node or switch difficulty. The Home hub remains accessible with Back and contains Daily, Free Play, Settings, Statistics, and Credits.

5. **Level preparation**  
   Before the first shot, the player sees the level number/name, objective, difficulty, shot limit, star targets, aim style, best result, and any newly introduced mechanic tips. The Start button begins the level.

6. **Gameplay**  
   Keep the objective/progress and remaining shots visible in the HUD. Preserve the existing aim path, wall bounces, next-bubble swap, precise aim, left-handed option, symbols, reduced motion, TalkBack actions, and board inspection. The first campaign level teaches in three small practice steps rather than presenting every instruction at once.

7. **Pause and results**  
   Pause offers Continue, Restart, Settings, and Exit. Win/loss results show the earned stars, score, shot count, and score breakdown, with a clear next-level/retry/map action.

8. **Return and progression**  
   Winning advances the current map marker and saves results locally. Returning to the map should make the next step obvious without forcing the player through the Home hub.

## Existing systems verified in source

- Native Android app using Kotlin and Jetpack Compose.
- Android minimum SDK 26; offline local catalogue and profile storage.
- Six original local WAV cues: launch, bounce, match, drop, victory, defeat. They are generated by `tools/generate_sounds.py`, not copied from another game.
- SoundPool playback and optional vibration; sound/haptics settings remain user-controlled.
- The existing game engine owns physics, aiming, collisions, match/drop rules, scoring, and saved campaign sessions. UI upgrades must not change these rules.
- Keep the MIT copyright/license notices and font license notices. Do not add competitor logos, sprites, characters, music, sounds, or other proprietary assets.

## Acceptance checklist for the map-first UX

- Fresh install opens the Map after the real loading step.
- Fresh profile selects Easy by default.
- Returning profile retains its saved difficulty.
- Saved campaign session exposes a Resume action from the Map.
- A new campaign level still opens its preparation screen before gameplay.
- Locked levels cannot start.
- Back from the Map opens the Home hub; Back from preparation returns to the Map.
- Existing tests, catalogue checks, certificate replay, lint, and debug/release builds must pass.
- Actual touch targets, layout, performance, sound, and gameplay must still be checked on a real Android phone.
